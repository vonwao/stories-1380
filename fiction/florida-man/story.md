# Florida Man, Corrected

Voice memo one. Testing. This is Ray Alvarez, recorded on my own phone in my own kitchen, with the refrigerator making that noise Linda says is normal and I say is a compressor begging for help.

Callie, you said I should tell the stories in order and stop correcting strangers in comment sections, because strangers in comment sections are not, quote, a jury of my peers. That was hurtful and accurate. So here we are.

The first headline is from 2018.

FLORIDA MAN STEALS TWELVE-FOOT ALLIGATOR FROM MINI GOLF COURSE.

Correction. It was fiberglass.

That matters. A live alligator has rights, or at least teeth. This was a fiberglass alligator named Captain Chompers from Treasure Lagoon Mini Golf, and I did not steal him in the spiritual sense. I moved him. The owner owed me one hundred and eighty dollars for pressure washing the pirate ship, the cave, and the blue whale, which is a lot of mildew shaped like family entertainment. He said the work was incomplete because I did not pressure wash the alligator. I said the alligator was not on the invoice. He said all exterior themed elements were implied.

So yes, after two beers and one YouTube video about small claims court that made it sound too slow, I loaded Captain Chompers into my truck.

This was wrong. I understand that now. I understood it then, but with less legal vocabulary.

The photo makes it look worse because the alligator's head is sticking out over the tailgate and I am giving a thumbs-up. I would like the record to show I was giving the thumbs-up to a man in a Jeep who yelled, "Hell yeah," which is not an argument but does feel like one at the time.

No one was protected in this story. There is no deeper wound. I was forty-six years old and mad about a fake alligator. We can move on.

Voice memo two.

FLORIDA MAN DRIVES GOLF CART ON INTERSTATE WITH GOAT IN PASSENGER SEAT.

Correction. It was not the interstate. It was the shoulder of a state road, which is still bad but not nationally bad. And the goat was not in the passenger seat. The goat was standing where the golf bags go, because it had eaten part of the seat the week before.

The goat's name is Lionel. Lionel belonged to a kid named Matty who lived two doors down from my sister back when my sister still had a yard. Matty raised him for 4-H and then found out, late in the process, what the H's were leading to. Nobody explained it to him correctly. Adults do this to children all the time. We use words like livestock and project because we are afraid of saying, you have been brushing the animal we plan to sell by the pound.

Matty called me crying because his mother was at work and because I am the kind of adult children call when the plan is already bad. I accept that about myself. It is not a compliment.

My truck was at Linda's because the starter was doing performance art. My neighbor's golf cart was in the driveway with the key in it, because the county is held together by trust and bad habits. I put Lionel in the back, told Matty I was taking him to a farm west of town, and made it eleven minutes before a sheriff's deputy asked me what I thought I was doing.

I told him the truth. He said, "Sir, that is not better."

The headline says I was wearing a bathrobe. That part is true. It was Matty's emergency, not mine, and emergencies do not wait for pants.

Lionel lived six more years at a place with two donkeys and a woman who called every animal baby. Matty is in college now. He pretends not to remember this story because he is nineteen and has a girlfriend. I am respecting that by not using his last name, even though everyone back there knows exactly which goat I mean.

Voice memo three.

FLORIDA MAN THROWS CHICKEN TENDERS AT CITY COMMISSIONER.

Correction. One tender. Singular.

Also, it missed.

This was at the public meeting about the marina leases. I had three minutes to speak, same as everyone, and I had written my comments on the back of a receipt from Chicken Kitchen because that was the paper in my truck. The commissioner, whose name I will not say because he has enough Google already, looked at his phone the entire time I was talking. Not glanced. Looked. Thumb moving.

I said, "Are you hearing any of this?"

He said, "Your time is still running."

That is when the tender left my hand.

For Callie and for any future employer who finds this audio after I die and thinks, "This man had impulse control issues": correct. But the tender did not leave my hand because he insulted me. I have been insulted by professionals. The tender left my hand because in the second row was Mrs. Alvarez, no relation, eighty-three, whose husband kept a skiff at that marina for thirty-one years and died in it with the radio on, which is probably the way he would have picked if anyone got to pick. She had spent the morning practicing her comments in English because she didn't want to use the translator. She got up there, said three sentences, and the commissioner looked at his phone.

So yes. Chicken tender.

Not proud. Not sorry in the clean way people want. Sorry for the throwing. Not sorry someone looked up.

Voice memo four.

FLORIDA MAN BREAKS INTO RETIREMENT HOME TO SING JOURNEY.

This one is almost completely false, except for the parts that are stupid.

It was assisted living, not a retirement home. I did not break in. The back door by the laundry room had been broken since Hurricane Irma, which I know because I fixed it twice and they kept replacing the good hinge with a cheap one. I did sing Journey. "Don't Stop Believin'." I am not proud of the key. It is too high for me and for most men with honest throats.

This was for Linda's aunt Mercy. Mercy had dementia, but not the movie kind where people become wise and say one perfect thing at sunset. Real dementia. Mean some days. Gone some days. Back for twenty seconds in the middle of lunch and furious about the soup. She had been a waitress at the old Jai-Alai fronton and could carry six coffee cups in one hand until arthritis took the trick away.

The facility locked down visitors after a flu outbreak. Reasonable. I am not anti-rule. The problem was that Mercy thought Linda had stopped coming. Every afternoon around four she asked where her niece was, and every afternoon someone told her Linda would come tomorrow, which was true in the administrative sense and false in the part of a person that keeps score.

Linda asked me to drop off a blanket. I dropped off the blanket. Then Mercy saw me through the laundry door and said, "Raymundo, sing the one from the bar."

No one calls me Raymundo except Mercy, and my mother when I have made a priest necessary.

So I sang. Quietly at first. Then less quietly because Mercy said, "Don't sing like you're stealing it." Three other residents came into the hall. One nurse filmed it, which is how the internet got involved. Security arrived during the second chorus, which is a merciful place to stop that song.

The headline made me sound like a lunatic. Fine. I have worn worse shirts than that. But Linda was embarrassed because Mercy's daughter saw the video from across the state and called yelling about dignity. Mercy had dignity. She also had the flu, dementia, and one song she could still ask for by insult. Sometimes dignity is overrated by people who have not been lonely at four in the afternoon.

Do not use Linda's last name. She asked me not to, and I am capable of learning eventually.

Voice memo five.

FLORIDA MAN SETS OWN BOAT ON FIRE DURING HURRICANE WARNING.

This is the one Callie told me not to record. She said, "Dad, maybe leave one alone." I considered it. I made coffee. I fed the neighbor's cat, which is not my cat no matter what Linda says. I sat here looking at the phone for twenty minutes.

The boat was a seventeen-foot Mako with a soft transom and a motor that started when it felt emotionally supported. I bought it from a man up the coast who said it needed love, which is a sentence that has cost me more money than any other sentence in English.

The hurricane was supposed to turn north. They always say that until they don't. I had the boat on the trailer in my driveway, tied down with straps that had seen better decades, when a woman I will call S. knocked on my door with two trash bags, a backpack, and her little boy holding a pillowcase full of dinosaurs. Not real dinosaurs, for the pedantic listener. Plastic.

S. was not my girlfriend. I know the comment section enjoyed that theory. She cut my hair for eight years at the shop by the Winn-Dixie and once told me my beard made me look like a divorced magician, which was unfair because I was only separated at the time. Her husband had rules about the car, the phone, the bank card, the weather, her mother, the boy's shoes. Men like that are very interested in rules. It makes them feel less like criminals.

She needed to get to her sister before the bridges closed. My truck had a full tank. We were loading the bags when his brother drove by slow.

Here is the part where people want bravery. I was not brave. I was angry and scared and already halfway into a bad idea. The boat was in the driveway. The brother knew the boat. Everybody knew the boat, because for six months I had been telling anyone with ears that I was going to fix it. So I rolled it into the street, poured gas in the bilge, and lit a rag with the grill lighter.

This is not recommended hurricane preparation.

The brother stopped for the fire. So did half the block. Fire has that old authority. While everybody watched my boat become a lesson, S. and the boy got in my truck through the passenger side. We left by the alley behind the duplexes. I drove them west in weather that made every overpass feel personal.

I told the police I was siphoning fuel and made a mistake. That is a stupid lie, but it is the kind of stupid lie that protects the person it is meant to protect. They charged me with careless burning and something about emergency conditions. The internet got the mugshot. My hair was bad because of the storm, not because of character.

S. is all right. The boy is in middle school now and apparently very tall. I know this because every Christmas a card comes with no return address and a picture of him next to something he has outgrown. Last year it was a bicycle. This year it was his mother.

I am not saying I am a good man. Good men do not solve problems by committing arson against their own recreational equipment. I am saying the headline is too small. They always are. That is the whole complaint. A headline is a door with no room behind it, and people love doors they never have to walk through.

So those are the corrections, Callie. Not innocence. I am not applying for innocence. I would just like the record to show that sometimes a man is ridiculous because he is ridiculous, and sometimes he is ridiculous because that is the only costume the truth can wear in public.

You can put this wherever you think it belongs.

But leave S. as S.
