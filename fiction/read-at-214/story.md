# Read at 2:14 a.m.

The first read receipt appeared under a message I'd sent four hundred and ninety-one days earlier. It was about a sandwich. Out of everything I wrote her — and I wrote her everything; there are six hundred and forty-one messages in that thread and she has answered exactly none of them — the one that finally got read was me describing a Cuban sandwich from a gas station outside Bethlehem, because it was the kind of thing she would have laughed at.

Under it, in small gray letters: **Read 2:14 a.m.**

The next morning there was a second one. The next message in the thread, in order. Her mother's roses had survived the freeze; I'd thought she should know. Read, 2:14 a.m.

One a day. In order. Always at 2:14.

Saturday my brother took me to the diner and laid his opinions out flat, the way he does, like tools on a towel.

"Delete the thread," he said. "It's been a year and a half. It's not a conversation."

"It was never a conversation." I wasn't arguing. Six hundred forty-one to nothing isn't a conversation; I know what it is. "It's a diary I keep in a place where she could find it."

"That's worse. You hear how that's worse?"

I put the phone on the table instead of answering. He scrolled without asking, which is a brother's whole job, and I watched him find the receipts, day after day, climbing the year like rungs.

"Her number got recycled," he said finally. "Some insomniac stranger is reading your mail."

"A stranger would start at the end. Whoever this is started at the beginning and reads one a day at the same minute." I'd had all week to think about it. "That's not curiosity. That's a ritual. Rituals are personal."

He looked at me the way you look at a man who has just said his one crazy thing out loud in a diner, and changed the subject to his transmission.

Here's what I didn't tell him. I don't sleep through 2:14 anymore. I set an alarm for 2:12 and lie in the dark holding the phone, and at 2:14, most nights, somewhere, a message I wrote when I still thought she was reading turns from delivered to read. Someone is going through my year the way you go through a shoebox of letters. It is the closest thing to being answered I've had in a long time, and I know how that sounds, and it might be enough anyway. Everyone keeps telling me I should want less. I've decided to stop apologizing for the size of what I want.

Last night I wrote message six hundred and forty-two. The first new one in months, and not to her, exactly.

*If you're going to read all of it, the good ones start in June.*

I spent forty minutes on it. I hadn't spent forty minutes on one since the beginning.

This morning it was marked read. Not in order. Whoever it is skipped four hundred messages to get to the new one, then went back to where they'd left off, like a person keeping their place with a finger.

2:14.
